The method

How the check works, and why we exclude paid links.

This page exists for the skeptical, technical buyer. No pitch. If you want to know exactly what happens when you type your domain into the box, it's here.

1 · The crawl

We read your published post archive — sitemap first, then the blog index if there's no sitemap. We cap the crawl at 200 URLs and cache the result by domain for 24 hours, so a refresh doesn't re-crawl. If your blog sits on a subdomain or renders entirely in JavaScript, the automated check may miss it; that's a warm lead, not a dead end, and we'll run it by hand.

2 · Referring domains, not backlinks

For each post we count referring domains — the number of distinct external sites that link to it — not raw backlink volume, which is trivial to inflate. A post linked 40 times from one scraper farm still scores a single referring domain. Zero referring domains is the invisible state, and it's the number the ledger surfaces.

3 · Placement fit scoring

In the paid audit, a human matches each defensible post to specific hosts, editors and reporters whose recent output has a gap the post fills. The FIT score is that reviewer's judgement of how directly your post answers something the outlet recently needed — with the reason written out, so you can overrule it.

4 · Why no paid links, ever

A link you pay for is a link that carries no independent judgement, ages badly, and — in regulated fields like law and dentistry — can be an undisclosed-consideration problem. Every placement we earn is an editorial decision by someone who could have said no, and often does. That's the whole asset. We do not buy, sell, or exchange links. Not as an upsell. Not ever.
